Sauerkraut
==========

+----------------------+------------------------+-------------------------+
| Prep Time: 5 minutes | Total Time: 40 minutes | Ferment Time: 3-10 Days |
+----------------------+------------------------+-------------------------+

Ingredients
-----------

- 1 medium head green cabbage (about 3 pounds)
- 1 1/2 tablespoons kosher salt
- 1 tablespoon caraway seeds (optional, for flavour)

Directions
----------

1. Clean everything to give the beneficial bacteria the best chance: wash and
   rinse the mason jar and jelly jar of all soap residue, then wash your
   hands, since you'll use them to massage the salt into the cabbage.
2. Discard the wilted, limp outer leaves of the cabbage. Cut the cabbage into
   quarters and trim out the core; slice each quarter down its length into 8
   wedges, then slice each wedge crosswise into very thin ribbons.
3. Transfer the cabbage to a big mixing bowl and sprinkle the salt over top.
   Massage and squeeze the cabbage with your hands until it turns watery and
   limp, more like coleslaw than raw cabbage, 5 to 10 minutes; at first it
   might not seem like enough salt. Mix in the caraway seeds now if using.
4. Pack handfuls of the cabbage into the canning jar, tamping it down with
   your fist every so often; a canning funnel makes this easier. Pour in any
   liquid released while massaging. Optional: place one of the larger outer
   cabbage leaves over the surface to help keep the cabbage submerged in its
   liquid.
5. Once all the cabbage is packed in, slip the smaller jelly jar into the
   mouth of the mason jar and weigh it down with clean stones or marbles to
   keep the cabbage submerged beneath its liquid.
6. Cover the mouth of the mason jar with a cloth and secure it with a rubber
   band or twine; this lets air flow in and out while keeping dust and insects
   out.
7. Over the next 24 hours, press down on the cabbage every so often with the
   jelly jar. As it releases its liquid it will become more limp and compact
   and the liquid will rise over the top.
8. If after 24 hours the liquid has not risen above the cabbage, dissolve 1
   teaspoon of salt in 1 cup of water and add enough to submerge the cabbage.
9. Ferment 3 to 10 days, away from direct sunlight at a cool room temperature,
   ideally 65F to 75F. Check daily and press it down if the cabbage is
   floating above the liquid. Because this is a small batch, it will ferment
   more quickly than larger batches. Start tasting after 3 days; when the
   sauerkraut tastes good to you, remove the weight, screw on the cap, and
   refrigerate. You can also let it continue fermenting for 10 days or even
   longer. There's no hard-and-fast rule for when it's "done", so go by how it
   tastes. While it's fermenting, you may see bubbles coming through the
   cabbage, foam on the top, or white scum. These are all signs of a healthy,
   happy fermentation. Skim the scum off the top either during fermentation or
   before refrigerating. If you see any mold, skim it off immediately and make
   sure the cabbage is fully submerged; don't eat moldy parts close to the
   surface, but the rest of the sauerkraut is fine. Store for several months:
   this sauerkraut is a fermented product, so it keeps for at least two months
   and often longer if kept refrigerated. As long as it still tastes and
   smells good to eat, it will be. If you like, transfer it to a smaller
   container for longer storage.

Notes
-----

- Sauerkraut with other cabbages: red cabbage, napa cabbage, and other
  cabbages all make great sauerkraut. Make individual batches or mix
  them up for a multi-coloured sauerkraut!
- Larger or smaller batches: keep the same ratio of cabbage to salt and
  adjust the size of the container. Smaller batches ferment more quickly
  and larger batches take longer.
- Hot and cold temperatures: do everything you can to store sauerkraut
  at a cool room temperature. At high temperatures it can sometimes
  become unappetizingly mushy or go bad. Low temperatures (above
  freezing) are fine, but fermentation will proceed more slowly.
