Guinness Beef Stew
==================

+-----------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------+
| Prep Time: 15 minutes | Cook Time: 2 1/2 - 3 hours | Yield: 4 - 6 servings |
+-----------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------+

Ingredients
-----------

- 6 pieces bacon, diced
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil
- 2.5 pounds (1.15 kg) beef chuck, boneless short rib or any other slow cooking beef, cut into approx 1 inch cubes
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- 3 carrots, cut into 1 inch pieces
- 14 ounces pearl onions, defrosted or 1 large yellow onion diced medium
- 6 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/4 cup tomato paste
- 3 tablespoons flour
- 8 ounces crimini mushrooms, stem trimmed and halved
- 1 1/2 cups chicken stock
- 1 (12-ounce / 355 ml) bottle Guinness (can substitute another stout or any dark beer)
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme, or 2 teaspoons dried
- 2 bay leaves

Directions
----------

1. Preheat oven to 325F.
2. Heat a heavy pot (that has a tight fitting lid) over medium heat and cook
   the bacon until just getting crispy, then remove and set aside. Leave a
   couple tablespoons of bacon grease in the pot, and add the beef in batches
   (so you don't overcrowd) until good colour develops on all sides, removing
   each batch and setting aside while you finish the rest.
3. Add the carrots and cook a couple minutes, then add the onion and stir to
   mix. Add the garlic, and when it's really fragrant add the tomato paste.
   Mix really well and let cook a couple minutes.
4. Sprinkle the flour over the top of everything and mix it in really well
   until everything looks gross and super dry. Gradually whisk in the stock,
   scraping the brown bits from the bottom of the pot. Add the beer, beef,
   bacon bits, and mushrooms, and mix well to combine. Add the thyme and bay
   leaves if using, put on the lid, and put in the oven for 2 hours.
5. When it's done, remove the lid and take out the thyme and bay leaves. Serve
   right away, or place back into the oven (uncovered) and cook another 30
   minutes to thicken, then serve over mashed potatoes or use in `Steak and Guinness Pie <steak-and-guinness-pie>`__.

Variations
----------

With Cheddar Herb Dumplings
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

A heartier, dumpling-topped version, adapted from
`Morgan <https://hostthetoast.com/guinness-beef-stew-with-cheddar-herb-dumplings/>`__.
Build the stew as above, with these changes: use 2 pounds boneless beef
chuck, add 4 chopped sticks of celery with the carrots and onion, and stir
in 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce with the Guinness. Along with the
beer and broth, plan to add 2 large diced potatoes (or parsnips) and 1
diced turnip partway through, and finish with chopped parsley.

- 4 sticks celery, chopped
- 2 large potatoes or parsnips, diced
- 1 turnip, diced
- 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch, or as needed
- Chopped parsley

Dumplings
^^^^^^^^^

- 1 1/2 cups self-rising flour
- 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1/3 cup shortening
- 3/4 cup shredded Irish sharp cheddar
- 2/3 cup milk
- 2 tablespoons mixed fresh herbs such as parsley, chives, and thyme,
  chopped

1. Simmer the stew covered for 1 1/2 hours, then add the potatoes (or
   parsnips) and turnip and simmer another 1/2 hour, until the vegetables
   are tender. Remove the bay leaves and thyme. If the stew is thin, stir
   in a slurry of 1 tablespoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon cold
   water, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer, add the mushrooms if
   using, and cook 10 minutes uncovered until thickened. Stir the bacon
   back in. Preheat the oven to 350F.
2. Stir together the self-rising flour and garlic powder in a medium bowl.
   Cut in the shortening until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs. Stir in
   the cheddar, then add the milk and stir until the dry ingredients are
   moistened.
3. Make small balls with the dough and place them on top of the stew,
   leaving room to expand. Bake the stew uncovered until the dumplings are
   browned and cooked through, about 30 to 40 minutes. Garnish with parsley
   and serve.
