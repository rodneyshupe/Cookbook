Instant Pot Glazed and Grilled Ribs
===================================

+-------------------+
| Yield: 4 servings |
+-------------------+

Pressure-cooking the ribs makes them completely tender, the perfect texture
for picking up and eating with your hands, with just the right amount of chew.

Equipment
---------
Electric Pressure Cooker

Ingredients
-----------

Ribs
^^^^

- 2 tablespoons mustard powder
- 1 tablespoon garlic powder
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1 1/2 tablespoons kosher salt
- 4 pounds (1.8 kg) pork spareribs, cut into 3- or 4-rib sections (you can also use beef back ribs)
- 1/4 cup mirin (sweet Japanese rice wine)

Glaze
^^^^^

- 2 tablespoons distilled white vinegar
- 2 tablespoons Sriracha
- 1 tablespoon fish sauce
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 2 3" pieces ginger, scrubbed, bruised with the dull side of a chef's knife, sliced into 1/2"-thick rounds
- Kosher salt (optional)

Directions
----------

1. Ribs: Mix mustard powder, garlic powder, sugar, and salt in a medium bowl
   to combine. Pat ribs dry with paper towels and arrange on a large rimmed
   baking sheet. Season both sides with all of the dry rub, patting it on if
   needed. Cover with parchment paper or loosely with plastic wrap and chill
   at least 1 hour or up to 2 days.
2. Pour mirin into the Instant Pot. Place ribs in the pot, orienting them on
   their narrow sides so they lean against the walls and each other rather
   than stacking flat. Lock on the lid, making sure the steam release valve is
   sealed. Select "Manual" and program for 9 minutes at high pressure. Let
   pressure release naturally 10 minutes, then manually release. Unlock the
   lid, transfer ribs to a rimmed baking sheet, and let sit at room
   temperature until ready to serve.
3. Do Ahead: Ribs can be cooked 1 day ahead. Let cool, then cover and chill.
4. Glaze and Assembly: Prepare a grill for medium-high heat, then clean and
   oil the grate. Pour cooking liquid from the Instant Pot through a fine-mesh
   sieve into a medium measuring glass, keeping the sieve handy for later.
   Using a spoon, skim excess fat from the surface; you should have about 1
   1/4 cups cooking liquid after skimming.
5. Pour cooking liquid into a large skillet. Add vinegar, Sriracha, fish
   sauce, and sugar and stir to combine. Add ginger, then bring to a boil.
   Cook, swirling the pan occasionally, until liquid is reduced by half, 7-8
   minutes. Strain glaze through the reserved sieve back into the measuring
   glass and set close to the grill.
6. Rub a little oil on the ribs to lightly coat. Grill, meaty side down, until
   lightly browned, about 2 minutes. Turn, generously brush glaze onto the
   exposed side, and grill until the underside is lightly browned, about 2
   minutes. Turn again and brush with glaze. Continue grilling, turning and
   brushing with glaze every 2 minutes, until ribs are dark brown and nicely
   lacquered on both sides with some charred spots, about 5 minutes total; the
   smaller end of the rack will be done first.
7. As they finish, transfer ribs to the same rimmed baking sheet and brush
   immediately with glaze. Let cool 5-10 minutes before cutting into
   individual ribs. Serve with any remaining glaze drizzled over, and season
   with salt if needed.
