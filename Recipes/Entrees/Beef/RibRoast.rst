Rib Roast
=========

+-----------------------+--------------------+--------------------------+---------------------+
| Prep Time: 15 minutes | Cook Time: 4 hours | Total Time: up to 7 days | Yield: 6-8 servings |
+-----------------------+--------------------+--------------------------+---------------------+

Ingredients
-----------

- 1 first-cut beef standing rib roast 7 pound (3.2kg) aprox. 3 bones
- 1 (3- to 4-bone) standing rib roast, preferably from the loin end, with the fat cap in place, 7 to 10 pounds (3.15 to 4.5 kg)
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil, to coat the roast, plus more if needed
- Kosher salt, 2 teaspoons per bone
- 1 tablespoon freshly ground black pepper

Directions
----------

1. Up to 7 days ahead, wrap the ribs in cheesecloth and place in the fridge;
   short on time, salt the ribs 2 days ahead and refrigerate wrapped loosely
   in paper towel.
2. When you're ready to roast, pull the roast from the fridge (leave it on
   its rack and half sheet pan) and rub with the oil, bones included, then
   once it's completely coated, cover with kosher salt, using about
   2 teaspoons per bone, and the pepper, and let sit at room temperature for
   1 hour.
3. Set a probe thermometer into the centre of the roast and set its alarm for
   118F, then put the roast on the lower middle rack in a cold oven and turn
   the oven to 250F, roasting until the meat hits its target, about 3 hours
   for a 3-bone roast or up to about 4 hours for a 4-bone roast. (Watch the
   temperature more than the clock; times vary with the exact weight and
   shape of the roast.)
4. When the alarm goes off, transfer the roast to a cutting board, cover with
   foil, and let it rest while you make the pudding (the internal temperature
   should climb to around 130F, then very slowly start to fall); pour the
   drippings from the sheet pan into a heat-proof liquid measuring cup, then
   rest the roast for 30 minutes, hanging on to the sheet pan and rack, as
   you'll use them again.
5. After 30 minutes, return the roast to the rack and sheet pan and back into
   the oven, crank the oven as high as it goes, ideally 550F but 500F works
   too, and cook until the exterior is browned, 10 to 15 minutes, depending
   on how hot your oven runs.
6. Return the roast to the board, carve, and serve with Yorkshire pudding.
   And no, you don't need to rest it again.
