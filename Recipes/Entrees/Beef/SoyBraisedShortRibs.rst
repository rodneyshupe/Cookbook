Soy-Braised Short Ribs
======================

+-----------------------------------+---------------------+-----------------------+
| Prep Time: 30 minutes + overnight | Cook Time: ~6 hours | Yield: 6 - 8 servings |
+-----------------------------------+---------------------+-----------------------+

Ingredients
-----------

- 5 pounds (2.26 kg) meaty short ribs on the bone, 2-3 inches thick
- Salt
- Neutral tasting oil, such as canola or grapeseed
- 1/4 cup (60 grams) soy sauce
- 1/4 cup (55 grams) dark brown sugar
- 1/4 cup (58 grams) mirin (rice wine)
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- 1 tablespoon finely grated fresh ginger
- 4 garlic cloves, finely grated or pounded with a pinch of salt
- 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
- About 1 cup (228 grams) dashi
- 1/4 cup coarsely chopped cilantro leaves and tender stems
- 4 scallions, green and white parts slivered

Directions
----------

1. Start the day before you want to cook. Season the meat lightly with salt
   and let it sit 30 minutes. The marinade is mostly soy sauce, which is
   salty, so use only about half as much salt as you otherwise would.
2. Meanwhile, whisk together the soy sauce, brown sugar, mirin, sesame oil,
   ginger, garlic, and cayenne. Put the meat in a resealable plastic bag,
   pour in the marinade, seal, and squish it around so all the meat is evenly
   coated. Refrigerate overnight.
3. A few hours before cooking, pull the ribs out of the fridge to come up to
   room temperature. Adjust the oven rack to the middle position and preheat
   to 400F.
4. Set a large cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. When the pan is hot,
   drop the heat to medium-low and add just enough oil to coat the bottom.
   Working in batches so you don't crowd the pan, brown a few ribs at a time
   on all three meaty sides, adding more oil as needed before each batch.
   Reduce the heat as necessary so they brown gradually; expect 4 to 5
   minutes per side for well-browned ribs.
5. Transfer the ribs bone-side down to a large Dutch oven or a deep ceramic
   or pyrex roasting dish, then pour the marinade over the meat. Snug is
   fine, but they should all fit in a single layer. Add enough dashi to come
   about 3/4-inch up the sides, then cover with a lid or parchment and
   aluminum foil. Cook until the liquid simmers, about 30 minutes, then
   reduce the heat to 325F and cook until the meat is very tender and falling
   off the bone, 3 to 4 hours more.
6. Uncover and crank the oven to 450F. Brush the ribs with the braising
   juices and cook until shiny and brown, about 8 to 12 minutes.
7. Serve warm, garnished with cilantro and slivered scallions.

Making Dashi
------------

1. Place the water and a 6" piece of kombu in a medium saucepan over
   medium-high heat. Just before it comes to a boil, remove the kombu and
   discard. Add a handful (1/2 cup) of dried bonito shavings and simmer 8
   minutes, then turn off the heat and let stand 8 minutes.
2. Pour the dashi through a fine-mesh strainer set in a 2- or 4-cup measuring
   cup. You should have 1 1/3 cups (304 grams) dashi. If you don't, add water
   (pouring it through the strainer holding the strained katsuobushi) to make
   up the amount needed. Keep chilled in the fridge and use within a day or
   so.
