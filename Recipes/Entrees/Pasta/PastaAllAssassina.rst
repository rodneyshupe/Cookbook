Pasta all'Assassina
===================

+-----------------------+------------------------+----------+
| Prep Time: 10 minutes | Total Time: 35 minutes | Yield: 4 |
+-----------------------+------------------------+----------+

Assassin's spaghetti from Bari in Puglia: dried spaghetti cooked in a tomato
passata broth using the 'risottata' method. The result is slightly burnt,
crispy caramelised spaghetti with an intense spicy tomato flavour.

Ingredients
-----------

- 12 ounces spaghetti
- 14 ounces tomato passata (tomato puree)
- 1-2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1/4 cup extra virgin olive oil
- 2-3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 1/2 teaspoons red chili flakes
- 1-2 teaspoons sugar (optional)
- salt and pepper (to taste)
- 2 tablespoons fresh basil, chopped (optional)

Directions
----------

1. Make a broth with water, 2/3 of the tomato passata/puree (1:1), and the
   tomato paste and salt. Bring to a boil and simmer until bright red and
   tasty but still a broth. Keep it hot when adding to the spaghetti, so it
   won't lower the pasta's cooking temperature.
2. In a cast iron pan, heat the olive oil, garlic, and red chili flakes until
   fragrant, then pour in the 1/3 of undiluted tomato passata/puree. Add 1
   teaspoon sugar to temper the acidity from the tomatoes.
3. Spread the passata over the whole pan with a wooden spoon and let it reduce
   and thicken slightly. Put the uncooked spaghetti in the pan, distributing
   the pasta so it lies on top of the sauce. The passata will spit when first
   poured into the pan, so take care not to get burnt.
4. Wait for the underside of the spaghetti to start caramelising before
   turning it over, carefully and a little at a time using a spatula. Let the
   other side start to crisp a little, then pour in a ladle of the hot tomato
   broth, adding it to the sides of the pan rather than over the spaghetti.
5. Once the spaghetti softens enough, move and stir it gently with a wooden
   spoon. Free any strands stuck to the bottom of the pan and let the tomato
   broth spread. When the broth has reduced and the spaghetti starts to stick
   again, add more broth.
6. Keep repeating this step, adding tomato broth and stirring, until the
   spaghetti is coated in the sauce and cooked. If you run out of tomato
   broth, add a little water. When finished, this dish is relatively dry and
   crispy, not saucy.
7. If using basil, stir in and serve topped with parmesan.
